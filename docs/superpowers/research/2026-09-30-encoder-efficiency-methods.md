# Efficiency methods for the Von option scorer (ModernBERT-large, ORT Web WebGPU)

*2026-09-30. Shape: prefix 150 + K≈50 × ~18 tokens ⇒ T≈1,100; 28 layers; measured 817 ms.*

## 0. Cost model

ModernBERT-large has ~342M non-embedding params (per layer Wqkv 1024×3072, Wo 1024², Wi 1024×5248, Wo_mlp 2624×1024) ⇒ ~684 MFLOP/token of linear work, **~750 GFLOP at T=1,100**. A masked export computes dense T×T attention at *every* layer (the 128-window is a mask, not a smaller kernel): ~5 GFLOP/layer, ~140 GFLOP. ~0.9 TFLOP in 817 ms ≈ **1.1 TFLOP/s effective**, far below any Apple GPU's fp32 peak — kernel-bound (fp32 dequant-in-loop MatMulNBits, unfused attention), not FLOP-bound. At M≈1,100 rows each weight byte is reused ~1,100× (~360 FLOP/B for Wi), so the matmuls are compute-bound: **weight bit-width barely affects latency, only download**. Unfused attention is the opposite: each layer round-trips a 16×T×T fp32 score tensor (77 MB) several times, ~12 GB/decision — memory-bound, plausibly 10–25 % of wall time. So the big levers are fewer token-layers (T × depth) and fp16 matmul kernels; the attention win is real but bounded.

## 1. Structural

**Prefix K/V reuse across options.** The packed sequence already carries the prefix once; reuse only helps if K forces chunking (each chunk repeats 150 tokens, ~+14 %). A two-pass graph is exact only if prefix tokens attend to the prefix alone; then prefix states feed ORT `MultiHeadAttention`'s `past_key/past_value` `(B,N,past,H)` [ContribOperators](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/main/docs/ContribOperators.md), which the native WebGPU kernel supports (it rejects `key_padding_mask` and packed QKV) [multihead_attention.cc](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/main/onnxruntime/contrib_ops/webgpu/bert/multihead_attention.cc). Gain ≤14 %. Skip.

**Option-side caching across steps (PreTTR split).** What persists between steps is the *options*, not the prefix. PreTTR runs the first *l* layers with the two sides blocked from each other, joins them for *l+1..n*, and caches the document side [MacAvaney 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14255). BERT-base: WebTrack P@20 within noise up to l=11/12; Robust04 nDCG@20 significantly down from l=8 (0.4357→0.4123, −5 %) and l=10 (0.4039, −7 %); latency 3.5× at l=8, 8.9× at l=10 (Tables 3–4). MICE (EMNLP'26) reaches the same conclusion from the other side: removing superfluous interactions cuts FLOPs 2.5× at equal effectiveness [MICE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16299). For us: split at l≈10–14/28; steady-state steps run lower layers on prefix + changed options only ⇒ ~1.7–2×/step (first step pays full). Training: block-diagonal mask in lower layers, fine-tune. Export: two graphs sharing weights, hidden states kept on GPU via `preferredOutputLocation:'gpu-buffer'` [ORT WebGPU tutorial](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/ep-webgpu.html). Risk medium (PreTTR's ERR@20 fell before P@20 — fine distinctions go first). ~2 d train + ~2 d runtime.

**Early exit / truncated depth with re-fitted head (incl. Matryoshka-style multi-exit).** MoSE (multi-exit 1B ModernBERT-style encoder): MRR 80.4 at layer 18/36 vs 81.0 full (−0.6) and 79.0 at 9/36 (−2.0) with self-distillation, vs 74.6 without [MoSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03008). LEAP: layer-aligned distillation *suppresses* the intermediate→final convergence early exit needs; exit-aware training gives 1.61× wall-clock [LEAP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01058). For ranking: early-exit monoBERT MRR@10 0.343→0.340 (−1 %) at 2.9×, −2 % at 3.2× [Xin 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.sustainlp-1.11.pdf); SEE up to 3.5× non-learned [SIGIR'25](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3726302.3729962). Ours: fixed exit L=14 ⇒ ~2×, L=16 ⇒ ~1.75×; the shipped graph is simply truncated (download shrinks too). Train exit heads at {8,14,20,28} with MoSE-style weighted losses + KL from the final head — this also yields the cascade head (§3). Adaptive per-call depth is a data-dependent graph switch; ship fixed graphs instead. Risk medium-low. 1–2 GPU-days.

**Depth/width pruning + distillation.** CoFi prunes layers, heads, FFN and hidden dims jointly for >10× at distillation-level GLUE accuracy in ≤20 GPU-h [Xia 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00408). Width pruning fights WebGPU tiling (keep dims multiples of 64); depth pruning is the case above.

**Distill into ModernBERT-base / Ettin-150M.** Base: 22 layers, hidden 768, GLU 2,304, ~110M non-embedding ⇒ ~3.1× fewer linear FLOPs, ~1.3× cheaper attention [ModernBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13663); Ettin-150M (same recipe, open data, ICLR'26) beats ModernBERT per size [Ettin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11412). A 162-run controlled study finds pairwise/listwise objectives with hard negatives give "gains comparable to moving up one backbone size tier" [Encoder-only CE study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03010) — the large→base gap is of the size objective + negatives recover; a soft-target ModernBERT-base student kept ~97 % of a far larger teacher's F1 [ref](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.12677). ~2.5–3×, download ~150 MB q8. Highest risk (P≈0.45), 2–4 GPU-days.

**Token pruning/merging.** LTP 2.1× FLOPs, <1 % GLUE loss [Kim 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00910); ToP 8.1× [ToP](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.14393). But the prefix is 14 % of T — pruning it is worthless. The useful variant is per-option pooling at a middle layer: "Layer-wise Token Compression" gets +25 % QPS (passages) / +116 % (documents), sometimes *beating* uncompressed [ref](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20683). Pool each option to ~5 tokens after layer 8: (8×1,100 + 20×400)/(28×1,100) ≈ 0.55 ⇒ ~1.8×, static shapes, trainable in-graph. Risk medium.

**Shorter option text.** 18→11 tokens ⇒ T≈700 ⇒ ~1.55× linear, ~2.5× attention. CROSS-JEM scores many short items jointly, exploiting token overlap, at >4× lower latency [CROSS-JEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09795) — DOM options repeat role/state words, so a fixed-vocabulary encoding instead of prose is the same idea. ~1 d re-render + fine-tune; ablate per dropped field.

## 2. Numerical / runtime (ORT Web 1.30)

**shader-f16.** Apple GPUs: 100 % availability (macOS 99.85 %) [web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgpu/features/shader-f16); Chrome ≥121 [ORT Web README](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/main/js/web/README.md). Chrome measured the HF text-embedding benchmark 3× faster with f16 than f32 on an M1 Max [Chrome I/O'24](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/io24-webassembly-webgpu-2). You run everything in fp32 — an fp16-activation export is the largest untried runtime lever (est. 1.5–2.5×). ORT 1.30 "enabled LayerNorm fusion to fix FP16 inference correctness" [1.30 notes](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/releases/tag/v1.30.0); keep head, final LN and softmax fp32 via an op block list if parity demands.

**Which EP runs.** The 1.30 npm `dist/` has three wasm flavours: `ort.webgpu.min.mjs` loads `…asyncify`, `ort.jspi.*` loads `…jspi`, the default `ort.min.mjs` loads `…jsep` (unpkg listing); the docs still say ORT Web "currently ships with `--use_jsep` for the browser" while both EPs can coexist [WebGPU EP doc](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/WebGPU-ExecutionProvider.html). It matters: JSEP MHA needs `attention_bias` exactly `(B,N,S,T)` (77 MB at T=1,100) and has no flash path [JSEP MHA](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/main/js/web/lib/wasm/jsep/webgpu/ops/multihead-attention.ts); the native EP takes a broadcast `(1,1,S,T)` bias inside one fused `FlashAttentionProgram` (fp32/fp16, Apple-tuned, bidirectional), gated only by unpacked QKV and `head_size % 4 == 0` (ours 64) [flash_attention.cc](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/main/onnxruntime/contrib_ops/webgpu/bert/flash_attention.cc). **Action:** `ort.env.webgpu.profiling={mode:'default'}` [perf diagnosis](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/performance-diagnosis.html); if no `FlashAttention` kernel appears you are on JSEP. Then emit `com.microsoft.MultiHeadAttention` nodes from the onnx-ir export with the in-graph mask folded into one additive `(1,1,T,T)` bias. Est. 1.1–1.3×, exact.

**Graph capture / static shapes.** Capture replays the command stream and needs fully static shapes with all kernels on WebGPU [WebGPU EP doc](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/WebGPU-ExecutionProvider.html). Bucket T to multiples of 64–128 so K maps to a few captured graphs; ~600 dispatches of CPU encoding vanish (likely 10–30 ms). IO binding only matters for the cascade.

**Subgroups / subgroup-matrix.** ORT's flash and MatMul kernels branch on `Subgroups` (shipped; check `adapter.features.has('subgroups')`). The 3× Apple prefill result (Phi-3.5, 1K tokens, 14.5→5.4 s) is `SubgroupMatrixMatMulNBits`, prefill-only and **restricted to `accuracy_level=4` (int8 activations)** [PR 23729](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/pull/23729); subgroup-matrix is still Dawn-experimental behind `--enable-unsafe-webgpu` — unavailable to an extension on stock Chrome.

**Int8 activations and the head.** With p=softmax(z/τ), δp ≈ p(1−p)·δz/τ: at τ≈⅓ every 0.01 of logit noise is 0.03 of probability — your 3×. The gate is a *calibration* gate, tighter than any accuracy gate: W4A4 is "no to negligible" loss for BERT on GLUE [Wu 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12017) yet fails yours. Fixes: (a) judge quantization on argmax agreement + KL and **re-fit τ afterwards** (noise mostly rescales logits); (b) if probabilities must survive, quantization-aware *distillation* with KL to the fp32 teacher — near-zero KL where CE-QAT is not [QAD](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20088) — GPTQ/AWQ init calibrated on real packed sequences, SmoothQuant migration for the post-LN outlier channels [SmoothQuant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438), last 4–6 layers + head fp16. But per §0, at M≈1,100 low-bit *weights* buy download, not latency, and int8 *activations* pay only with packed-int8 dot hardware (DP4a 1.7–2.9× over f16 on Intel [Chrome I/O'24](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/io24-webassembly-webgpu-2); Metal unverified). Low priority for Apple latency.

**WebNN.** Origin trial M147–M149, no Android/Safari/Firefox; macOS is Core ML with GPU/NPU behind a further flag [webnn.io](https://webnn.io/en/api-reference/browser-compatibility/api), [status](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/frontier-web-apis-2026-production-ready). Not shippable in 2026.

## 3. Algorithmic

**Pre-filter.** MindAct uses a separate DeBERTa-base ranker: recall@50 = 88.9/85.3/85.7 % [Mind2Web](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06070) — a separate small model loses 11–15 % of targets even at K=50; lexical filters are worse for actions ("Submit" rarely appears in the instruction). Better: an **in-model** cascade — the layer-8 exit head shortlists top-16, layers 9–28 run on prefix + 16 (T≈440): (8×1,100 + 20×440)/(28×1,100) ≈ 0.57 ⇒ ~1.75×, exact whenever recall@16 holds. Runtime: two sessions, GPU-resident hidden states, ONNX `Gather` with indices as input.

**Bi-encoder / late interaction.** ColBERT approaches monoBERT at ~70× lower cost on MS MARCO [Lin survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06467) and stays competitive in 2025 comparisons [ref](https://dev.to/gabrielanhaia/reranker-selection-cross-encoder-vs-llm-as-reranker-vs-colbert-which-earns-its-latency-2h7o), but element choice depends on siblings/role/state in a way passage relevance does not. Use late interaction only as a shortlist tower (option encodings cached across steps; MaxSim is free), never to decide; hybrid shortlist-16 → cross-encoder at T≈440 ⇒ ~2.5× on today's kernels. Measure recall@16 offline first.

## 4. Attention-mask cost

Unfused: memory-bound (~12 GB score traffic/decision; 30–170 ms across M1→M-Max bandwidths) plus the masks if materialised per head. Fused native `FlashAttention` with bias: compute-bound, ~7–15 % of FLOPs. Block-sparse/varlen: ORT's `PackedMultiHeadAttention` is CUDA-only; its WebGPU paged/varlen kernels are causal decoder paths (`PagedAttention`, GQA `local_window_size`) [ContribOperators](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/main/docs/ContribOperators.md), [PR 32462](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/pull/32462) — nothing does bidirectional segment-id attention on WebGPU. Practical route: dense fused kernel + one broadcast bias. Algorithmic route: Set-Encoder-style per-option rows with positions reset to 0 and cross-talk only through the prefix — attention becomes K×L_opt×(P+L_opt), ~7× fewer FLOPs, batched MHA with no mask at all (a training change).

## 5. Relevant 2025–26 work

- **Set-Encoder** (ECIR'25): passages as separate sequences, positions reset, `[INT]` tokens the only inter-passage channel; matches pointwise (0.733 vs 0.720 nDCG@10 DL'19) and LLM listwise rerankers without significant difference [Schlatt](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06912v3). Template for a mask-free set scorer.
- **CROSS-JEM**: joint short-item scoring, >4× lower latency [ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09795). **jina-reranker-v3**: "last but not late" — all candidates in one context, per-document final-token embedding, one pass (61.85 BEIR at 0.6B) [ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25085); our pattern on the decoder side.
- **MICE** 2.5× FLOPs [ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16299); **RRK** listwise over compressed docs, 3–18× faster than 0.6–4B rerankers [ref](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26483); **Layer-wise Token Compression** [ref](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20683); **SEE** early exit [ref](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3726302.3729962); **LEAP** [ref](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01058); **MoSE** [ref](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03008).
- Generative lineage: ListT5 (FiD + tournament sort) [ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15838), FIRST (first-token logits — the decoder analogue of a `[MASK]` head) [ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15657), Rank-without-GPT [ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02969).
- Training recipe: hard negatives + listwise objective ≈ one size tier [ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03010); Ettin as student family [ref](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11412).

| # | Method | Speedup (T≈1,100) | P(≤1 pt) | Cost | Accept when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | fp16 export + confirm native EP + graph capture on bucketed T | 1.5–2.5× | 0.85 (re-fit τ) | 1–2 d | fp16 kernels in profile; max\|Δp\| ≤ 0.03 after τ re-fit |
| 2 | Fused `MultiHeadAttention` + `(1,1,T,T)` bias | 1.1–1.3× | ~1.0 | 1–2 d export | `FlashAttention` in profile; parity vs unfused |
| 3 | Shorter option encoding (~11 tokens, role vocab) | 1.5× | 0.7 | 1 d data + FT | grounding acc; per-field ablation |
| 4 | Multi-exit self-distillation, ship truncated L=14–16 | 1.75–2× | 0.65 | 1–2 GPU-d | exit acc within 1 pt; ECE unchanged |
| 5 | In-graph cascade: layer-8 head → top-16 → upper layers | 1.6–1.9× (stacks) | 0.7 | 2–3 d | recall@16 ≥ 0.99; end-to-end acc |
| 6 | Distill into ModernBERT-base / Ettin-150M | 2.5–3× (+download ÷4) | 0.45 | 2–4 GPU-d | listwise-KL student within 1 pt |

**Order.** (1)+(2) first — no training, exact or near-exact, multiplies everything after; expect ~817→350–450 ms at K=47. Then (3) in the next data render. Then one training run that adds exit heads (4) *and* yields the cascade head (5); ship L=16 truncated if it passes, else L=28 + cascade. Projected 350 × 1/1.5 × 1/1.75 ≈ 130–170 ms at K≈50 without touching model size. Keep (6) as fallback — the only lever that also fixes the 616 MB download. Skip 4-bit/int8 activations for latency, prefix K/V caching, and WebNN.

**Measurement:** `ort.env.webgpu.profiling` per-kernel before/after; `scripts/system1-eval/` for p50 decide and grounding; parity max|Δp| on the calibration set *after* τ re-fit; ECE at the 0.4/0.6 bands so the tentative/free split keeps its meaning.
